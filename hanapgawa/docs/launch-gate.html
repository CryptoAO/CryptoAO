Perspective sweep · regulatory

The Philippine regulatory picture

Ordered by how badly each bites. This is preparation for counsel, not a substitute for counsel — confirm each with a Philippine lawyer and accountant before the first real peso.

FOUNDER
Business registration DTI/SEC, Mayor's permit, BIR 2303. Nothing operates lawfully or issues a receipt without it.
COUNSEL
The wallet-versus-e-money question — the most dangerous item on this page Stored value users top up and hold is e-money under BSP rules unless the funds sit with a licensed EMI. The design already keeps funds with the aggregator; the product's "wallet" framing must match the legal structure. Two clean paths: contract so the aggregator legally holds the stored value, or drop stored value for authorize-and-capture per booking. Decide with counsel before launch — this choice shapes the payment integration.
FOUNDER
NPC registration Appoint the Data Protection Officer and register the processing systems with the National Privacy Commission (Circular 2022-04). Government IDs and precise locations at scale make this mandatory, and the DPO's name fills the placeholder in the privacy notice.
COUNSEL
Internet Transactions Act (RA 11967) E-marketplace obligations and DTI E-Commerce Bureau registration. A services marketplace should assume it is in scope until counsel says otherwise.
NOT DONE
BIR withholding on payouts (RR 16-2023) Platforms withhold 1% on one-half of gross remittances to sellers above de-minimis thresholds — the rule that made Shopee sellers register with BIR, and it covers services. Build the withholding and Form 2307 pipeline before payout volume is real.
WITH SMS SETUP
SMS sender-ID registration Telcos drop unregistered A2P SMS since the SIM Registration Act, and an OTP that does not arrive is a dead app. The aggregator handles registration — with lead time, so start it the day the SMS account opens.
DESIGNED · WATCH
Worker classification Providers are independent contractors, and the product keeps the employer firewall: no imposed schedules, no exclusivity, providers set rates. Watch the pending platform-worker bills — a misclassification finding converts every provider into an employee retroactively.
DONE
RA 10173 operations Consent recorded with version, data export and account closure self-service, the 72-hour breach clock in the runbook.
Perspective sweep · the people

Provider, client, security, ease of use

PROVIDER
Built: the reasons to stay Guaranteed payment with auto-release, SOS and check-ins, proof of income, suki fee cuts, the activation checklist, availability that is actually enforced.
PROVIDER
Missing: SMS fallback and an appeal path A provider off data for a day must still learn they won a booking — wire booking-won and SOS to SMS once the account is live. And a flagged provider needs a way to tell their side without hunting for a support email. Same-day cash-out becomes real only with live payout rails; it is the number providers quote to each other.
CLIENT
Cash-in friction is the #1 conversion killer Requiring a funded wallet before booking loses the client with ₱500 in GCash and zero in the app. Per-booking GCash payment must be the first live-rails milestone — it also softens the e-money question. Cebuano before expanding past Tagalog-region cities.
SECURITY
The one thing worth paying for: an independent penetration test Two adversarial review rounds ran and their findings are fixed, admin logins now take a second factor — but a platform whose promise is "hindi ka maloloko dito" does not launch on self-review. Budget ₱150–400k, before marketing, after live rails.
EASE
Twenty real people in one city, on their own phones Still the most important unbuilt thing. Every assumption — escrow tolerance, masked chat, badge trust — is untested against a real labandera. Expect assisted sign-ups (the kapatid model) and watch for them.
Always-on invariants

What must be checked, and stay true, at all times

The launch checklist every app needs, sized for one founder. These are never allowed to silently stop being true.

HIGHEST RISK
Backups that restore Automated database backups plus a practiced restore. A backup you have never restored is a hope, not a backup — and losing the ledger loses the business. Cheapest fix on this page: an afternoon of configuration in the hosting account.
EMITS · UNWIRED
Monitoring that pages a human The app ships a health probe and scrubbed error reporting; nothing receives them yet. An external uptime check on /api/health plus a live error DSN closes it.
SMALL
A support channel users can find Even just a monitored inbox linked from the safety page. A trust platform with no way to reach a human is a contradiction users will notice first.
RUNBOOK
Daily money reconciliation Ledger totals against the aggregator statement, every day, from day one. The append-only ledger makes it a query; the habit has to exist before the discrepancy does.
PLACEHOLDERS
Accurate legal pages Terms and privacy notice are live and consent is recorded — but the bracketed company name and DPO must be filled before a single real user.
DONE
Incident and breach plan · CI · weekly dependency audit The runbook covers the 2am SOS call, dispute defaults, and the 72-hour NPC clock. CI gates every push; a weekly audit catches vulnerable dependencies without reddening PRs.
LATER
Kill switches and a staging environment Pausing sign-ups or bookings without a deploy, and somewhere to deploy that is not production. Acceptable gaps at pilot scale where a deploy takes minutes; close both before marketing spend.
The moat

How a non-novel idea becomes a novel business

Escrow, KYC and masked chat are table stakes — nobody wins by having them, they only lose by lacking them. The novelty comes from what Facebook groups structurally cannot do. Four theses, in conviction order.

Thesis 1 · shipped

Patunay ng Kita — the platform as a credit history

An informal worker's deepest unmet need is not finding work; Facebook finds work. It is that ten years of real income leaves no record a bank will accept. Every completed job here is an escrow ledger row, and the platform now turns that ledger into a verifiable earnings statement a loan officer checks in ten seconds. The pitch flips from "maghanap ka ng trabaho dito" to "bawat trabaho mo ay pumapasok sa record na tatanggapin ng bangko." Facebook can never offer this. Long game: banks accept the statement formally, then lenders underwrite against it — at which point leaving the platform costs a provider their credit history.

Thesis 2 · the wedge to build next

The OFW wedge — paying from abroad for care at home

An OFW in Dubai books and pays for aircon cleaning at their parents' house in Iloilo. The payer has digital money and cannot be scammed by a stranger at their mother's door — exactly what escrow, vetted IDs, evidence photos and check-ins were built for. Two million deployed OFWs remitting ~$37B a year, digitally paying-capable and trust-motivated. Needs only international cards (the aggregator has them), a "book for someone else" field, and marketing in OFW communities. "Alaga mo sila, kahit malayo ka." No incumbent owns this.

Thesis 3 · shipped

Suki economics

Western marketplaces fight the repeat relationship; here it is the culturally strongest force in service commerce, so the platform rewards it — the take rate falls 12% → 10% → 8% as a pair completes jobs together, cutting the fee exactly when leak risk peaks. With Thesis 1, staying pays twice: cheaper fees and a growing credit record. A switching cost that compounds with every completed job.

Thesis 4 · shipped, needs telling

Safety as the brand

No incumbent leads with worker safety. SOS that writes first and asks questions never, trusted contacts, check-ins, evidence photos, a runbook whose first rule is call the person before the money. "Ang app na may kasamang bantay" — a marketing asset as much as a feature; providers recruit providers on it.

The growth road, as requested: direct request-to-book and browse-by-availability shipped this pass. Instant book (provider opt-in, skips confirmation inside stated hours) follows once real-user testing shows confirmation friction costs bookings — the rails already exist. Organization accounts — agencies posting and serving as businesses — are next on the roadmap and deliberately not bolted on: they change the trust model (DTI/SEC verification instead of a personal ID, per-worker vetting so a badge still refers to the human who shows up, dispatch, org-level reputation, different BIR treatment). The clean entry: pilot with two or three real cleaning teams in one city as design partners before generalizing.

Sequence

The order of operations

Each step is worth little until the one before it is done.

  1. Founder, this week: backups, a support inbox, the legal-page placeholders, and start business + NPC registration.

    All paperwork and configuration; no code is waiting on any of it.

  2. The wallet decision, with counsel.

    It shapes the payment integration, so it precedes it.

  3. Wire live rails: SMS with sender-ID registration, then PayMongo.

    Cash-in friction, same-day payouts, and SMS fallbacks all unblock at once.

  4. Twenty real users in one city.

    Before more code. Every further build decision should be downstream of what they do.

  5. Independent penetration test.

    After rails are live, before marketing spend.

  6. RR 16-2023 withholding pipeline.

    Before payout volume makes it a compliance debt.
